Cargo Claims Policy & Procedure

Effective Date: June 1, 2026  |  3 Lakes Logistics LLC  |  State of California

TIME-SENSITIVE NOTICE: Cargo claims have strict statutory deadlines. Under the Carmack Amendment (49 U.S.C. § 14706), a written claim must be filed with the carrier within nine (9) months of delivery (or the date delivery should have occurred). Failure to file within this deadline may permanently bar recovery. Contact 3LL immediately upon discovering cargo loss or damage.

SECTION 1 — PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1 Purpose

This Cargo Claims Policy and Procedure document establishes the framework for reporting, processing, and resolving cargo loss and damage claims involving freight arranged by 3 Lakes Logistics LLC ("3LL"). This policy applies to all carriers, owner-operators, shippers, and consignees who have a business relationship with 3LL and applies to all shipments arranged or brokered by 3LL under any freight agreement or rate confirmation.

1.2 Scope

This policy covers:

  • Complete or partial loss of cargo during transit;
  • Physical damage to cargo during loading, transit, or unloading;
  • Shortage (missing units or pieces not accounted for at delivery);
  • Concealed damage discovered after delivery;
  • Delay claims (where applicable under the shipping contract);
  • Contamination or temperature deviation claims (reefer loads);
  • Any other loss or damage to freight for which a carrier may bear liability.

SECTION 2 — LEGAL FRAMEWORK: THE CARMACK AMENDMENT

2.1 Carmack Amendment Overview

Interstate cargo claims are governed primarily by the Carmack Amendment to the Interstate Commerce Act, codified at 49 U.S.C. § 14706. Under the Carmack Amendment:

  • A motor carrier is liable for the actual loss or injury to property transported in interstate commerce;
  • Liability attaches from the time the carrier receives the shipment in good condition until delivery;
  • The carrier bears the burden of proving that loss or damage was caused by one of the recognized defenses (Act of God, act of shipper, public enemy, act of public authority, or the inherent nature of the goods);
  • Liability may be limited by the carrier's tariff or a written agreement between the parties (such as a rate confirmation or broker-carrier agreement).

2.2 Filing Deadline — Nine (9) Months

A written cargo claim must be filed with the responsible carrier within nine (9) months from: (a) the date of delivery; or (b) the date delivery should have reasonably occurred if the cargo was lost or undelivered. Failure to file within this deadline permanently bars the claimant from recovering for the loss or damage, regardless of the merits of the claim.

2.3 Civil Suit Deadline

If the carrier denies the claim or fails to respond within thirty (30) days, the claimant must file a civil lawsuit within two (2) years from the date the carrier disallows the claim in writing. This deadline is separate from and in addition to the nine (9) month filing deadline.

2.4 Default Liability — $0.50 Per Pound

Unless a higher liability limit is expressly stated in the applicable carrier agreement or rate confirmation, 3LL's standard carrier agreements and rate confirmations establish a default liability limit of $0.50 per pound of the lost or damaged commodity. Carriers seeking higher coverage should arrange cargo insurance that covers the full value of the shipments they transport. Shippers who require full-value coverage should notify 3LL in writing prior to tendering the shipment.

IMPORTANT: The $0.50/lb default limit may not adequately cover the full value of high-value or specialized commodities. Shippers and carriers should review all applicable agreements and obtain appropriate insurance coverage.

2.5 Intrastate Claims

For intrastate shipments entirely within California, claims may be governed by California state law rather than (or in addition to) the Carmack Amendment. The same general principles regarding carrier liability, timely notice, and documentation apply.

SECTION 3 — CARRIER OBLIGATIONS

3.1 Pre-Trip Inspection

Before accepting a load, the carrier/driver must:

  • Inspect the freight at the point of pickup and note any pre-existing damage, shortage, or discrepancy on the Bill of Lading (BOL) before signing;
  • Count all pieces and verify that the count matches the BOL;
  • Inspect the trailer/container for cleanliness, odors, temperature (reefer), and structural integrity;
  • Document any exceptions with written notations on the BOL and, if possible, with photographs;
  • Obtain the shipper's countersignature on any BOL exceptions noted;
  • Notify 3LL of any concerns, discrepancies, or exceptions before departing the pickup location.

3.2 During Transit

During transit, the carrier shall:

  • Maintain safe driving practices and comply with all applicable HOS regulations;
  • Monitor temperature settings on reefer units and document temperature logs;
  • Secure cargo in accordance with FMCSA cargo securement standards (49 CFR Part 393);
  • Immediately notify 3LL of any accident, breakdown, theft, or other event that may have caused or may cause cargo damage or delay;
  • Take all reasonable steps to mitigate potential cargo damage, including seeking assistance, rerouting, or re-icing as appropriate.

3.3 Delivery Obligations

At the point of delivery, the carrier/driver shall:

  • Obtain a signed delivery receipt from the consignee;
  • Allow the consignee adequate time to inspect the freight before signing the delivery receipt;
  • Note any visible damage, shortage, or discrepancy on the delivery receipt and obtain the consignee's signature on any exceptions;
  • Immediately notify 3LL if the consignee refuses delivery or notes significant damage;
  • Retain all delivery documentation and provide copies to 3LL upon request.

3.4 Notification Requirements

The carrier must notify 3LL within the following timeframes:

Event Required Notification Timeframe
Accident or incident during transit Immediately (within 1 hour)
Cargo discovered damaged during transit Immediately upon discovery
Theft or suspected theft Immediately; law enforcement report required
Temperature deviation (reefer) Immediately upon detection
Consignee notes damage at delivery Within 2 hours of delivery
Concealed damage reported by consignee Upon notification from consignee; preserve access for inspection

SECTION 4 — CLAIM SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS

4.1 Who May File a Claim

A cargo claim may be filed by: (a) the shipper (consignor); (b) the consignee (receiver); (c) the owner of the cargo; or (d) 3LL on behalf of any of the above, as authorized in writing. Claims should be submitted to 3LL in the first instance so that 3LL can assist in processing the claim with the responsible carrier.

Every cargo claim submission must include the following documents. Incomplete claims may delay processing:

2 Original Bill of Lading (BOL) — signed by both shipper and carrier Establishes condition at pickup
3 Delivery Receipt / Proof of Delivery (POD) — with any noted exceptions Establishes condition at delivery
4 Commercial Invoice or packing list for the damaged/lost cargo Establishes value of claim
5 Photographs of damage (at minimum: packaging, item, trailer, and point of impact) Visual evidence of damage
6 Rate Confirmation or applicable freight contract Establishes carrier identity and liability terms
7 Repair estimate or salvage value documentation (if applicable) Quantifies actual damages
8 Weight ticket or manifest (if weight is a factor) Establishes weight-based liability limits

10 Police or incident report (theft/accident claims) Official documentation of event
11 Salvage receipts (if cargo was sold for salvage value) Documents mitigation efforts

4.4 Photograph Requirements

Photographs should be taken as soon as damage is discovered and must include:

  • Wide-angle shots of the entire pallet or load;
  • Close-up shots of specific areas of damage;
  • Photographs of the packaging (outer and inner if accessible);
  • Photographs of any BOL or label markings;
  • Photographs of the trailer interior (including walls, floor, and any other relevant areas);
  • If applicable: photos of temperature recorder, reefer unit settings, seals, and securement devices.

SECTION 6 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION

6.1 Claim Denial

If a carrier denies a cargo claim or offers a settlement that the claimant finds unacceptable, the claimant may:

  • Request a written explanation of the denial from 3LL;
  • Provide additional documentation or evidence to support the claim;
  • Request that 3LL advocate on the claimant's behalf in negotiations with the carrier;
  • Pursue mediation through a neutral third party acceptable to both Parties;
  • File a civil action against the responsible carrier in a court of competent jurisdiction, subject to the two (2) year statute of limitations from the date of written denial.

6.2 3LL's Role in Dispute Resolution

3LL's role in cargo claims is that of a logistics intermediary and advocate. 3LL will make reasonable efforts to facilitate communication between the claimant and the carrier, assist in gathering documentation, and advocate for a fair resolution. However, 3LL is not the carrier and does not bear primary liability for cargo loss or damage unless separately agreed in writing. 3LL's efforts in claim assistance do not constitute an assumption of carrier liability.

6.3 Governing Law for Disputes

Claims governed by the Carmack Amendment shall be subject to federal law. All other disputes between 3LL and any claimant arising under this policy shall be governed by California law and resolved in California state or federal courts.

SECTION 7 — MITIGATION OBLIGATIONS

All parties to a cargo claim are obligated to take reasonable steps to mitigate their losses. This includes:

  • Carriers: Taking immediate action to prevent further damage, refrigerating temperature-sensitive cargo, contacting 3LL for instructions when in doubt, and not abandoning damaged freight without authorization;
  • Shippers/Consignees: Documenting and preserving all damaged cargo; not disposing of damaged cargo until authorized by 3LL and/or the carrier; obtaining fair market salvage value if cargo must be disposed of; and providing timely access for inspection.

Failure to mitigate may reduce the recoverable amount by the extent to which additional damages could have been avoided.

SECTION 8 — HOW 3LL ASSISTS WITH CLAIM FILING

3 Lakes Logistics LLC provides the following claim assistance services to shippers and carriers:

  • Documentation Assistance: Helping claimants identify and gather all required documentation;
  • Claim Preparation: Assisting in the completion of the Cargo Claim Form and ensuring all required information is included;
  • Carrier Communication: Forwarding claims to the responsible carrier and following up on the carrier's investigation and decision;
  • Negotiation Support: Advocating on behalf of claimants in negotiations with carriers for fair settlement;
  • Insurance Coordination: Where applicable, coordinating with 3LL's contingent cargo insurer for claims that may be covered under 3LL's policy;
  • Deduction Authorization: Where a carrier is responsible and the amount is undisputed, deducting the claim amount from the carrier's settlement and remitting to the claimant, subject to the carrier's consent or applicable legal authority.
