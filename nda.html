RECITALS

WHEREAS, the Company is engaged in the business of freight brokerage, logistics coordination, transportation dispatch, carrier management, and related services, and possesses certain confidential, proprietary, and trade secret information relating to its business operations, customers, carriers, technology, and financial affairs;

SECTION 1 — DEFINITION OF CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION

1.1 Scope of Confidential Information

For purposes of this Agreement, "Confidential Information" means any and all information, data, or material, in whatever form or medium, that relates to the Company's business, operations, technology, personnel, customers, carriers, or financial affairs, and that is not generally known or available to the public. This includes but is not limited to the following categories:

Category Examples
Carrier Information Carrier lists, preferred carrier databases, carrier contact information, carrier rates and agreements, carrier performance data, owner-operator information, MC/DOT numbers associated with established carrier relationships
Rate and Pricing Data Shipper rates, carrier pay rates, margin data, lane-specific pricing, fuel surcharge structures, accessorial rate schedules, rate confirmation details, pricing strategies and models

Trade Secrets Information that derives independent economic value from not being generally known or readily ascertainable, including business methods, operational procedures, competitive strategies, and market analysis
Personnel Information Employee and contractor compensation, performance evaluations, personal contact information, organizational structure, and staffing plans
Business Operations Load data, shipment tracking information, dispatch logs, operational procedures, compliance documentation, vendor agreements, and business development strategies
Strategic Information Business plans, expansion plans, acquisition targets, marketing strategies, competitive intelligence, and other forward-looking information

1.2 Form of Confidential Information

Confidential Information includes information disclosed in any form, whether written, electronic, digital, oral, visual, or any other medium. Confidential Information disclosed orally shall be identified as confidential at the time of disclosure; provided, however, that information that a reasonable person in the industry would understand to be confidential given the context shall be treated as Confidential Information regardless of whether it is expressly labeled as such.

1.3 Third-Party Confidential Information

Confidential Information also includes confidential information of third parties (including customers, carriers, and partners) that has been provided to the Company under obligations of confidentiality and that the Company shares with the Receiving Party in the course of the Receiving Party's engagement.

SECTION 2 — OBLIGATIONS OF THE RECEIVING PARTY

2.1 Duty of Confidentiality

The Receiving Party agrees to:

  • Hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence and treat it with at least the same degree of care that the Receiving Party uses to protect the Receiving Party's own confidential information, but in no event less than a reasonable standard of care;
  • Not disclose, share, transfer, publish, communicate, or otherwise make available any Confidential Information to any third party without the prior written consent of the Company;
  • Use the Confidential Information solely and exclusively for the purpose of performing services for, or in connection with the Receiving Party's authorized business relationship with, the Company;
  • Not use any Confidential Information for the Receiving Party's own benefit or for the benefit of any third party, competitor, or other adverse interest;
  • Implement and maintain appropriate technical, physical, and organizational safeguards to prevent unauthorized access to, disclosure of, or use of Confidential Information;
  • Promptly notify the Company in writing if the Receiving Party becomes aware of any actual or suspected unauthorized disclosure or use of Confidential Information.

2.2 Restrictions on Internal Disclosure

The Receiving Party shall not disclose Confidential Information to any person except to those of the Receiving Party's employees, agents, or subcontractors who: (a) have a legitimate need to know the Confidential Information for the purposes of the authorized engagement with the Company; (b) have been informed of the confidential nature of the information; and (c) are bound by confidentiality obligations no less restrictive than those contained in this Agreement. The Receiving Party shall be responsible for any breach of this Agreement by any such person to the same extent as if the Receiving Party had committed such breach directly.

2.3 No Reverse Engineering

The Receiving Party shall not reverse engineer, disassemble, decompile, or attempt to derive the source code, algorithms, processes, ideas, or structures of any software, technology, or proprietary system included in or derived from the Confidential Information.

2.4 No Unauthorized Copying

The Receiving Party shall not copy, reproduce, or duplicate any Confidential Information except as reasonably necessary to perform the Receiving Party's obligations under the applicable engagement with the Company. Any copies or reproductions shall be subject to the same confidentiality obligations as the original.

2.5 Security Incidents

If the Receiving Party becomes aware of any security breach, unauthorized access, or other incident involving Confidential Information, the Receiving Party shall: (a) notify the Company in writing as soon as practicable (and in no event later than twenty-four (24) hours after becoming aware); (b) provide all reasonably available details regarding the incident; (c) cooperate fully with the Company in investigating and mitigating the incident; and (d) take all reasonably necessary steps to prevent further unauthorized access or disclosure.

SECTION 3 — EXCEPTIONS TO CONFIDENTIALITY OBLIGATIONS

3.1 Exceptions

The obligations of confidentiality set forth in this Agreement shall not apply to information that the Receiving Party can demonstrate by clear and convincing evidence:

  • (a) Public Domain: Is or becomes generally available to the public through no act or omission of the Receiving Party or any person for whom the Receiving Party is responsible;
  • (b) Prior Knowledge: Was known to the Receiving Party prior to disclosure by the Company, as evidenced by written records or other tangible documentation in existence prior to the date of disclosure;
  • (c) Independent Development: Was independently developed by the Receiving Party without reference to, use of, or reliance upon any Confidential Information, as evidenced by written records created independently of and prior to such disclosure;
  • (d) Rightful Third-Party Disclosure: Was received by the Receiving Party from a third party who had no restriction on disclosure with respect to such information and who did not obtain such information directly or indirectly from the Company;
  • (e) Required Legal Disclosure: Is required to be disclosed by applicable law, regulation, judicial or administrative order, or governmental authority, provided that the Receiving Party: (i) gives the Company prompt written notice of such requirement as far in advance as practicable; (ii) cooperates with the Company's efforts to seek a protective order or other appropriate relief; (iii) discloses only that portion of the Confidential Information legally required to be disclosed; and (iv) uses reasonable efforts to ensure that the information is treated confidentially by the receiving authority.

3.2 Burden of Proof

The burden of establishing any exception under Section 3.1 rests solely upon the Receiving Party. The availability of information in the public domain does not, by itself, establish that the Receiving Party came to know such information from a public source rather than from the Company's disclosure.

SECTION 4 — OWNERSHIP; NO LICENSE

4.1 Ownership

All Confidential Information shall remain the exclusive property of the Company (or, in the case of third-party confidential information, of the applicable third party). Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed as granting the Receiving Party any right, title, interest, or license in or to any Confidential Information beyond the limited right to use such information as expressly permitted herein.

4.2 No License

This Agreement does not grant the Receiving Party any license under any patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret, or other intellectual property right of the Company, whether by implication, estoppel, or otherwise.

SECTION 5 — TERM AND DURATION

5.1 Term During Engagement

This Agreement shall remain in full force and effect during the entire period of the Receiving Party's employment, engagement, partnership, or business relationship with the Company, commencing on the Effective Date.

5.2 Post-Engagement Obligations — Three-Year Survival

The confidentiality and non-use obligations of the Receiving Party under this Agreement shall survive the termination, expiration, or conclusion of the Receiving Party's engagement or relationship with the Company and shall continue in full force and effect for a period of three (3) years following the date of such termination, expiration, or conclusion.

5.3 Trade Secret Protection — Perpetual

Notwithstanding the three (3) year post-engagement term, the Receiving Party's obligations with respect to any information that constitutes a "trade secret" under the California Uniform Trade Secrets Act (Cal. Civil Code § 3426 et seq.) or the federal Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. § 1836 et seq.) shall continue in perpetuity, or for so long as such information retains its status as a trade secret under applicable law, whichever is shorter.

SECTION 6 — RETURN OR DESTRUCTION OF CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION

6.1 Return or Destruction Obligation

Upon the termination or expiration of the Receiving Party's engagement or relationship with the Company, or upon the Company's written request at any time, the Receiving Party shall, within five (5) business days:

  • Return to the Company all Confidential Information and all materials (in any medium) containing, summarizing, or based upon Confidential Information in the Receiving Party's possession or control;
  • Permanently delete and destroy all copies, notes, summaries, analyses, or extracts of Confidential Information from all electronic devices, storage media, cloud storage, email accounts, and other locations; and
  • Provide the Company with a written certification confirming that all Confidential Information has been returned or permanently destroyed as required hereunder.

6.2 Backup Systems

To the extent the Receiving Party cannot reasonably delete Confidential Information from routine automated backup systems, the Receiving Party shall identify such backup systems, ensure they are not accessed for the purpose of retrieving Confidential Information, and permanently delete such Confidential Information from backup systems at the next available opportunity in the normal course of backup system management.

SECTION 7 — REMEDIES FOR BREACH

7.1 Irreparable Harm — Injunctive Relief

The Receiving Party acknowledges and agrees that:

  • Any actual or threatened breach of this Agreement would cause irreparable harm and damage to the Company for which monetary damages alone would be an inadequate remedy;
  • The Company shall be entitled to seek injunctive relief, specific performance, and other equitable remedies from any court of competent jurisdiction, without the necessity of posting a bond or other security and without proving actual damages; and
  • Such equitable remedies shall be in addition to, and not in lieu of, any other legal or equitable remedies available to the Company.

7.2 Monetary Damages

In addition to injunctive and equitable relief, the Company shall be entitled to recover all monetary damages caused by the Receiving Party's breach of this Agreement, including but not limited to lost profits, lost business opportunities, harm to goodwill, and costs of investigation and remediation, including reasonable attorneys' fees.

7.3 Attorneys' Fees

In any action or proceeding to enforce this Agreement, the prevailing party shall be entitled to recover its reasonable attorneys' fees and costs from the non-prevailing party, as permitted by applicable law.
